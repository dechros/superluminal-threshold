# The Arrow of Time is Alive and Well but Forbidden Under the Received View of Physics

**Authors:** R. E. Kastner

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11456 - arXiv abstract, full text not retrieved

## Abstract

This essay offers a meta-level analysis in the sociology and history of physics
in the context of the so-called "Arrow of Time Problem" or "Two Times Problem,"
which asserts that the empirically observed directionality of time is in conflict
with physical theory. I argue that there is actually no necessary conflict between
physics and the arrow of time, and that the observed directionality of time is
perfectly consistent with physics unconstrained by certain optional metaphysical,
epistemological and methodological beliefs and practices characterizing the
conventional or Received View.

## Why this matters here

Section 8.4 eliminates the metric as the carrier of the direction of time, and the
question that follows is where the direction may legitimately be sought. This
paper is useful precisely because it is not on the side one might expect. It
accepts that the received formalism carries no direction and argues that this is a
consequence of methodological choices rather than of physics, so the direction can
be restored by revising those choices rather than by finding it in the equations.

Two uses. It states the standard position sharply enough to cite: under the
received view, the directionality of time is in conflict with physical theory
because the theory does not contain it. That is the same elimination Section 8.4
reaches by calculation, arrived at from the philosophy of physics rather than from
a congruence. And it names the escape route, which is to locate the direction in
something the received view sets aside. This work's escape route is different and
narrower, being the entropy of the exit channels of Section 8.7, so the comparison
is a real one and not an appeal to authority.
