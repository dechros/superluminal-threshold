# Spontaneous photon production in time-dependent epsilon-near-zero materials

**Authors:** A. Prain, S. Vezzoli, N. Westerberg, T. Roger, D. Faccio

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08228 - arXiv abstract page only; full text not retrieved

## Abstract

Quantum field theory predicts that a spatially homogeneous but temporally varying medium will excite photon pairs out of the vacuum state. However, this important theoretical prediction lacks experimental verification due to the difficulty in attaining the required non-adiabatic and large amplitude changes in the medium. Recent work has shown that in epsilon-near-zero (ENZ) materials it is possible to optically induce changes of the refractive index of the order of unity, in femtosecond timescales. By studying the quantum field theory of a spatially homogeneous, time-varying ENZ medium, we theoretically predict photon pair production that is up to several orders of magnitude larger than in non-ENZ time-varying materials. We also find that whilst in standard materials the emission spectrum depends on the time scale of the perturbation, in ENZ materials the emission is always peaked at the ENZ wavelength. These studies pave the way to technologically feasible observation of photon pair emission from a time-varying background with implications for quantum field theories beyond condensed matter systems and with potential applications as a new source of entangled light.

## Why this matters here

Prain, Vezzoli, Westerberg, Roger and Faccio compute the quantum field theory of a spatially homogeneous but time varying medium and show that it excites photon pairs out of the vacuum, then identify a material class where the effect is large enough to see. The obstacle to observing this standard prediction has always been that one needs a non-adiabatic index change of order unity, and epsilon-near-zero materials permit exactly that on femtosecond timescales, which they estimate raises pair production by several orders of magnitude over ordinary time varying media. They also find a qualitative difference: in a standard material the emission spectrum tracks the timescale of the perturbation, whereas in an ENZ medium the emission is always peaked at the ENZ wavelength, that is at the frequency where the dispersion relation degenerates. That degeneracy is the useful part for us. A signature change is a degeneracy of the metric, the point where the effective refractive index of the background passes through the value that makes the dispersion relation change character, and this paper says that emission piles up precisely at such a point rather than being spread over the spectrum. It therefore predicts that particle creation at our threshold is not a small correction but the dominant channel near the degenerate frequency, which strengthens the reading that the returning quantum may be a created excitation and gives a concrete spectral signature to look for in our own Bogoliubov coefficients.
