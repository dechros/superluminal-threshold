# Quantum Theory of Wave Scattering from Electromagnetic Time Interfaces

**Authors:** M. S. Mirmoosa, T. Setälä, A. Norrman

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15178 - arXiv abstract page only; full text not retrieved

## Abstract

Modulating macroscopic parameters of materials in time offers innovative avenues for manipulating electromagnetic waves. Due to such enticing prospects, the general research subject of time-varying systems is expanding today in different branches of electromagnetism and optics. However, compared with the research efforts and progresses that have taken place in the realm of classical electrodynamics, the quantum aspects of this emerging subject have been less explored. Here, through the lens of quantum optics, we study the scattering of electromagnetic fields from an isotropic and nondispersive material with a suddenly changing refractive index, creating a time interface. We revisit the transformation of the bosonic mode operators and corresponding quantum states due to this interface, governed by the two-mode squeeze operator. Building on this foundation, more importantly, our analysis focuses on the photon statistics and quantum state engineering of the scattered light, elucidating various quantum optical phenomena and opportunities arising from the time interface. Notably, these include photon-pair production and destruction, photon bunching and antibunching, vacuum generation, quantum state discrimination, and quantum state freezing. To bridge theory and experiment, we propose a possible circuit quantum electrodynamics approach for validating our theoretical predictions. We hope that our work inspires experimental investigations and further quantum optical explorations of electromagnetic field interaction with photonic time crystals or with dispersive time-varying materials.

## Why this matters here

This supplies the Bogoliubov machinery for an abrupt discontinuity in the background, with the caveat that the matching condition is not ours: a time interface conserves the wavenumber while our spatial slab conserves the frequency, so the transfer of these results has to be argued rather than assumed. Mirmoosa, Setala and Norrman treat an isotropic nondispersive medium whose refractive index changes suddenly, and show that the transformation of the bosonic mode operators across the time interface is generated by a two-mode squeeze operator coupling the forward and backward modes at the same wavenumber. Because a time interface conserves the wavenumber and not the frequency, the outgoing state at a given k is a two-mode squeezed vacuum built on the incoming one, so the Bogoliubov alpha and beta coefficients are hyperbolic cosine and sine of a squeezing parameter fixed by the index ratio. They work out the consequences for photon statistics: pair production and also pair destruction depending on the incoming state, bunching and antibunching, vacuum generation, quantum state discrimination and state freezing, and they propose a circuit QED realisation. The relevance to us is direct and slightly uncomfortable. If the returning excitation in our setup is produced by mode mixing at the entry and exit surfaces, then what comes back is one member of a created pair, correlated with a counter propagating partner, and its arrival statistics are those of a squeezed state, not those of the original particle. Our frequency mixing that keeps the thickness information is the same beta coefficient seen from the classical side, and this paper says what its quantum counterpart is.
