# Comment on "Macroscopic violation of special relativity" by Nimtz and Stahlhofen [arXiv:0708.0681v1]

**Authors:** Herbert G. Winful

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.2736 - arXiv abstract page only; full text not retrieved

## Abstract

A recent paper by G. Nimtz and A. A. Stahlhofen [arXiv:0708.0681v1] makes the following claims: (1) that the authors have observed a macroscopic violation of special relativity, (2) that they have demonstrated quantum mechanical behavior of evanescent modes on a meter-length scale, and (3) that barriers are crossed in zero time, implying superluminal (faster than light), and indeed, infinite tunneling velocity. Here I suggest that all these claims are erroneous and are based on a misinterpretation of a purely classical measurement accurately described by Maxwell's equations.

## Why this matters here

The direct half of the Nimtz debate, and worth having in the folder together with Nimtz's own statement so the article can present the dispute rather than one side of it. Nimtz and Stahlhofen had claimed a macroscopic violation of special relativity from a double prism frustrated total internal reflection experiment, with barriers crossed in zero time and hence infinite tunnelling velocity. Winful replies that the measurement is entirely classical, fully described by Maxwell's equations, and that the observed beam shift is a lateral Goos-Haenchen type displacement in the transverse direction rather than a longitudinal transit across the gap, so no time is being measured along the tunnelling direction. He also points out that the zero time claim follows only if one insists on reading a saturating delay as a transit time. For our purposes this fixes the standard of evidence: an advance that shows up only as a shifted peak, without an independent front or an independent clock, is not by itself evidence that anything crossed faster. The article should not cite Nimtz style zero time claims as support without also carrying this rebuttal.
