# Do single photons tunnel faster than light?

**Authors:** Herbert G. Winful

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.3889 - arXiv abstract page only; full text not retrieved

## Abstract

Experiments done in the early 1990's produced a surprising result: that single photons pass through a photonic tunnel barrier with a group velocity faster than the vacuum speed of light. Subsequent experiments with classical pulses have also revealed apparent superluminal group velocities as well as tunneling times that saturate with barrier length, a phenomenon known as the Hartman effect. In this paper we show that the measured delays are in fact cavity lifetimes as opposed to transit times. This interpretation resolves the Hartman paradox and shows that tunneling is not superluminal as widely believed.

## Why this matters here

The short companion to Winful's group delay analysis, aimed at the Steinberg, Kwiat and Chiao single photon experiments and at the frustrated total internal reflection experiments that reported Hartman saturation. Winful argues that what those experiments measure is a cavity lifetime, the barrier acting as a low quality resonator whose stored field leaks out of both ends, and not a transit time across the barrier width. The key move is that the reported superluminal group velocity is obtained by dividing the barrier width by a quantity that is not a traversal time, so the ratio has no kinematic meaning. This matters here because our observable is exactly such a ratio in disguise: we read the return moment off the phase of a two-crossing amplitude and then compare it with the free flight time over the same coordinate distance. It also raises a concrete question for the intermediate region of our setup, where the mode is not evanescent and no stored-field reading is available, so the Winful objection may be regime dependent rather than global. Presenting both this and the group delay paper gives the article the strong form of the objection in the exact experimental cases the Hartman literature keeps citing.
