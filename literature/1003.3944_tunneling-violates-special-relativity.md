# Tunneling Violates Special Relativity

**Authors:** Guenter Nimtz

**Journal:** Foundations of Physics, 41, 1193-1199 (2011)

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.3944 - arXiv abstract page only; full text not retrieved

## Abstract

Experiments with evanescent modes and tunneling particles have shown that i) their signal velocity may be faster than light, ii) they are described by virtual particles, iii) they are nonlocal and act at a distance, iv) experimental tunneling data of phonons, photons, and electrons display a universal scattering time at the tunneling barrier front, and v) the properties of evanescent, i.e. tunneling modes is not compatible with the special theory of relativity.

## Why this matters here

Nimtz's compact statement of the position that the tunnelling advance is a genuine signal effect, published in Foundations of Physics, and the natural counterweight to the Winful papers. He asserts five things: that the signal velocity of evanescent modes may exceed c, that evanescent modes are the virtual particles of the process, that they are nonlocal and act at a distance, that phonon, photon and electron data all show a universal scattering time at the barrier front of order 1 / nu with nu the carrier frequency, and that these properties are not compatible with special relativity. The universal front time claim is the empirically sharpest of the five and is the one that would matter for us: it says the delay is set by the carrier frequency rather than by the barrier, which is a different saturation law from the stored-energy law Winful predicts, and the two can in principle be told apart in our own numerics. Nimtz's signal velocity claim, if it survived, would mean that our advanced return moment carries information. The claim is disputed by essentially everyone else in this list, and the article should treat it as a minority position that is nonetheless clearly stated and testable.
