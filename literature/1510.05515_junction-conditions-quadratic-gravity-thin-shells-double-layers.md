# Junction conditions in quadratic gravity: thin shells and double layers

**Authors:** Borja Reina, José M. M. Senovilla, Raül Vera

**Journal ref:** none

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.05515 - arXiv abstract listing

## Abstract

The junction conditions for the most general gravitational theory with a Lagrangian containing terms quadratic in the curvature are derived. We include the cases with a possible concentration of matter on the joining hypersurface -termed as thin shells, domain walls or braneworlds in the literature- as well as the proper matching conditions where only finite jumps of the energy-momentum tensor are allowed. In the latter case we prove that the matching conditions are more demanding than in General Relativity. In the former case, we show that generically the shells/domain walls are of a new kind because they possess, in addition to the standard energy-momentum tensor, a double layer energy-momentum contribution which actually induces an external energy flux vector and an external scalar pressure/tension on the shell. We prove that all these contributions are necessary to make the entire energy-momentum tensor divergence-free, and we present the field equations satisfied by these energy-momentum quantities. The consequences of all these results are briefly analyzed.

## Why this matters here

Reina, Senovilla and Vera derive the junction conditions for the general Lagrangian quadratic in curvature, covering both the proper matching, where only finite jumps of the energy-momentum tensor are allowed, and the case with matter concentrated on the joining hypersurface. Two results matter here. First, the proper matching conditions in quadratic gravity are strictly more demanding than in General Relativity, so moving to a higher curvature theory does not open a route to a layer-free crossing, it closes routes. Second, when a shell is present it is generically not a plain Israel shell: it carries a double layer contribution in addition to the usual surface energy-momentum tensor, and that double layer induces an external energy flux vector and an external scalar pressure or tension on the surface. The authors prove that every one of these pieces is needed for the total energy-momentum tensor to be divergence free, and they write down the field equations the surface quantities obey. For our work this fixes the vocabulary and the bookkeeping for surface content, and it warns that any attempt to cancel our one over thickness layer by adding curvature-squared terms will generate a dipole-type layer instead of nothing.
