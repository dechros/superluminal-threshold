# Born's Rule for Arbitrary Cauchy Surfaces

**Authors:** Matthias Lienert, Roderich Tumulka

**Journal:** Lett. Math. Phys. 110, 753-804 (2020)

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.07074

## Abstract

Suppose that particle detectors are placed along a Cauchy surface Sigma in Minkowski space-time, and consider a quantum theory with fixed or variable number of particles (i.e., using Fock space or a subspace thereof). It is straightforward to guess what Born's rule should look like for this setting: The probability distribution of the detected configuration on Sigma has density |psi_Sigma|^2, where psi_Sigma is a suitable wave function on Sigma, and the operation |.|^2 is suitably interpreted. We call this statement the "curved Born rule." Since in any one Lorentz frame, the appropriate measurement postulates referring to constant-t hyperplanes should determine the probabilities of the outcomes of any conceivable experiment, they should also imply the curved Born rule. This is what we are concerned with here: deriving Born's rule for Sigma from Born's rule in one Lorentz frame (along with a collapse rule). We describe two ways of defining an idealized detection process, and prove for one of them that the probability distribution coincides with |psi_Sigma|^2. For this result, we need two hypotheses on the time evolution: that there is no interaction faster than light, and that there is no propagation faster than light. The wave function psi_Sigma is obtained from the Tomonaga-Schwinger equation, or from a multi-time wave function by inserting configurations on Sigma. Thus, our result establishes in particular how multi-time wave functions are related to detection probabilities.

## Why this matters here

This supplies the missing empirical link in the multi-time to single-time reduction: it shows that the object obtained by evaluating the multi-time wave function on a chosen surface Sigma is not merely a formal restriction but the thing whose modulus squared gives detection probabilities on that surface. The reduction map is stated explicitly, psi_Sigma is obtained by inserting configurations lying on Sigma into phi(x_1,...,x_N), with the density built using nu(x) = n_mu(x) gamma^mu, the matrix formed from the future unit normal of Sigma at x. That normal vector is the local orientation of the chosen time direction, so the paper makes the orientation of the reduction surface appear directly inside the probability formula, which is exactly the dependence our project is after. The derivation shows the curved Born rule follows from the flat one plus a collapse rule, provided two conditions hold: no interaction and no propagation faster than light. Those two hypotheses are precisely what our superluminal interface violates, so this result also marks where the standard reduction argument stops applying and tells us which assumption must be replaced when the region beyond the threshold is reached. Read together with the multi-time consistency literature, it means that shifting the surface changes the detection statistics through n_mu, while the underlying multi-time state is unchanged.
