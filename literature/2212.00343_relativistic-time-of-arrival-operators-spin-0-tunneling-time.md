# Quantized relativistic time-of-arrival operators for spin-0 particles and the quantum tunneling time problem

**Authors:** Philip Caesar Flores, Eric A. Galapon

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00343 - arXiv abstract page only; full text not retrieved

## Abstract

We provide a full account of our recent report (EPL, 141 (2023) 10001}) which constructed a quantized relativistic time-of-arrival operator for spin-0 particles using a modified Weyl-ordering rule to calculate the traversal time across a square barrier. It was shown that the tunneling time of a relativistic spin-0 particle is instantaneous under the condition that the barrier height $V_o$ is less than the rest mass energy. This implies that instantaneous tunneling is an inherent quantum effect in the context of arrival times.

## Why this matters here

The closest existing construction to what our project requires: a time of arrival observable for a relativistic spin-0 particle, that is for a Klein-Gordon field with real mass, which is our field exactly. Flores and Galapon quantize the classical relativistic arrival time using a modified Weyl ordering rule, obtaining a time of arrival operator whose expectation gives the traversal time across a square barrier without appealing to any proper time or internal clock. Their result is that the tunnelling time of a relativistic spin-0 particle is instantaneous provided the barrier height V_o is below the rest mass energy, and they argue this instantaneity is an intrinsic quantum feature of arrival times rather than an artefact of a particular time definition. Two things follow for us. First, the construction shows how to define the return moment for a Klein-Gordon packet in a region where no proper time is available, which is exactly the situation inside a signature-changed slab, so the method transfers even though the background does not. Second, their conclusion is stronger than ours: where we find a delay that saturates with thickness, they find zero traversal time under a stated condition on the barrier height relative to mc^2, and our own saturation should be compared against that limit, since a saturating but nonzero delay and an exactly vanishing one are different claims about the same regime.
