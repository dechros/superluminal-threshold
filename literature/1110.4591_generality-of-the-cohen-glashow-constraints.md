# On the generality of the Cohen and Glashow constraints on the neutrino velocity

**Authors:** F. L. Villante, F. Vissani

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.4591 - arXiv abstract, full text not retrieved

## Abstract

We discuss the kinematic limits for the process ν_μ → ν_μ + e^+ + e^- in the
assumption that neutrinos are superluminal. We derive our results by assuming
that: i) it exists one reference frame in which energy and momentum are
conserved; ii) the Hamilton-Jacobi equation v = dE/dp is valid; iii) the present
experimental information on the neutrino velocity at different energies are
correct. We show that the considered process cannot be avoided unless very
peculiar neutrino dispersion laws are assumed.

## Why this matters here

This is the empirical wall, and the only paper in the corpus that is one. Every
other objection in the article's register is internal: a junction condition, an
energy condition, a well-posedness failure. This one says that a superluminal
particle radiates, that the radiation has been looked for, and that it was not
seen.

The mechanism is vacuum Cherenkov emission. A particle moving faster than the
phase velocity of the fields it couples to is above threshold for pair emission
into the vacuum, so it bleeds energy continuously and cannot arrive. Cohen and
Glashow applied this to the OPERA claim; the contribution here is to show how
little the argument assumes. Three hypotheses carry it: some frame in which
energy and momentum are conserved, the Hamilton-Jacobi relation between velocity
and the derivative of energy with respect to momentum, and the measured
velocities themselves. No field content, no interaction Lagrangian, no
particular Lorentz-violating model. The escape the authors leave open is
correspondingly narrow: a dispersion law peculiar enough to close the phase
space.

Its bearing on the article is sharper than on the tachyon literature it was
written against, because the article's particle is not permanently superluminal.
It crosses a threshold, spends a while on the far side, and comes back. That
changes what has to be shown, and the article has not shown it:

1. **Is the crossing itself above threshold?** Cohen-Glashow applies to a
   particle propagating superluminally through a region where the ordinary
   fields live. The article's particle does not do that; it changes signature.
   Whether the emission channel exists during the crossing depends on what the
   fields do at the surface, and Section 3.4's layer is exactly where that
   question lives.
2. **The far side has no stable vacuum**, by the article's own Section 10.3, so
   "emission into the vacuum" has no referent there. That is an escape from this
   constraint, but it is an escape bought with a defect the article already
   counts against itself.
3. **The narrow exit the authors leave — a peculiar dispersion law — is one the
   article can actually inspect.** Section 9.1 computes the far-side dispersion
   and group velocity in closed form. Whether that dispersion, read back through
   the threshold, closes the pair-emission phase space is a calculation the
   article has the machinery for and has not run.

Until one of those three is settled the article has no answer to the only
experimental objection in its bibliography, and should say so rather than cite
the paper in passing.

Status against the article: CHALLENGE, empirical, unanswered.
