# The meaning of group delay in barrier tunneling: A re-examination of superluminal group velocities

**Authors:** Herbert G. Winful

**Journal:** New J. Phys. 8, 101 (2006) http://stacks.iop.org/1367-2630/8/101

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0601085 - arXiv abstract page only; full text not retrieved

## Abstract

We show that the group delay in tunneling is not a traversal time but a lifetime of stored energy or stored probability escaping through both ends of the barrier. Because it is a lifetime associated with both forward (transmitted) and backward (reflected) fluxes, it cannot be used to define a group velocity for forward transit in cases where a wavepacket is mostly reflected. For photonic tunneling barriers the group delay is identical to the dwell time which is also a property of an entire wave function with reflected and transmitted components. Theoretical predictions and experimental reports of superluminal group velocities in barrier tunneling are re-interpreted.

## Why this matters here

This is the single strongest challenge to the reading that our two-crossing Wigner phase delay is a transit time. Winful shows that the group delay in tunnelling equals the dwell time, and that for an opaque barrier the dwell time is not a time of flight at all but the lifetime of the energy stored in the evanescent region, tau_d = U / (P_in), the stored probability divided by the incident flux. Because the stored probability escapes through both faces, the same tau_d governs the transmitted and the reflected channel, so it cannot be converted into a one-way velocity D / tau when almost everything is reflected. On this reading the Hartman saturation is not a paradox and needs no explanation: the stored energy under an opaque barrier saturates with thickness because the evanescent field decays as exp(-kappa x), so the lifetime it defines saturates too, and the apparent velocity D / tau_phi grows without bound simply because the numerator grows while a length-independent quantity sits in the denominator. Applied to our setting this says that the saturation we compute for the two-crossing amplitude may be a property of the stored field in the signature-changed region rather than of any particle that went there and came back. The paper also predicts that the delay is set by the barrier's decay constant kappa and the coupling at the faces, so it should be reproducible from a stored-norm calculation in our own code as an independent cross-check. If our numerically computed phase delay matches U / P_in in the barrier regime, the Winful reading applies to us directly and the article must say so.
