# Time-of-arrival probabilities and quantum measurements: II Application to tunneling times

**Authors:** Charis Anastopoulos, Ntina Savvidou

**Journal:** J. Math. Phys. 49, 022101 (2008)

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.2492 - arXiv abstract page only; full text not retrieved

## Abstract

We formulate quantum tunneling as a time-of-arrival problem: we determine the detection probability for particles passing through a barrier at a detector located a distance L from the tunneling region. For this purpose, we use a Positive-Operator-Valued-Measure (POVM) for the time-of-arrival determined in quant-ph/0509020 [JMP 47, 122106 (2006)]. This only depends on the initial state, the Hamiltonian and the location of the detector. The POVM above provides a well-defined probability density and an unambiguous interpretation of all quantities involved. We demonstrate that for a class of localized initial states, the detection probability allows for an identification of tunneling time with the classic phase time. We also establish limits to the definability of tunneling time. We then generalize these results to a sequential measurement set-up: the phase space properties of the particles are determined by an unsharp sampling before their attempt to cross the barrier. For such measurements the tunneling time is defined as a genuine observable. This allows us to construct a probability distribution for its values that is definable for all initial states and potentials. We also identify a regime, in which these probabilities correspond to a tunneling-time operator.

## Why this matters here

This is the arrival time observable our project needs, formulated so that it does not presuppose a clock carried by the particle. Anastopoulos and Savvidou treat tunnelling as a time of arrival problem at a detector a distance L beyond the barrier, using a positive operator valued measure for the time of arrival that depends only on the initial state, the Hamiltonian and the detector position. That gives a genuine normalised probability density over arrival times, with every quantity unambiguously interpreted, which is exactly what a bare phase derivative does not give. Their result is favourable to the phase time in a limited way: for a class of localised initial states the detection probability identifies the tunnelling time with the classical phase time, so our Wigner phase reading is recoverable as a limit of an operational observable rather than being merely a formal derivative. They also establish limits to the definability of tunnelling time outside that class, and then extend to a sequential setup in which an unsharp phase space sampling precedes the barrier, where the tunnelling time becomes a genuine observable with a distribution defined for all initial states and potentials, and identify a regime in which those probabilities correspond to a tunnelling time operator. For us the practical import is that the return moment should be defined at a detector at fixed position outside the signature-changed region, and that the width of the arrival distribution, not only its mean, is part of the answer.
