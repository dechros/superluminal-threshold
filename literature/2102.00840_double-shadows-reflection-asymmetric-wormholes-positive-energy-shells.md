# Double shadows of reflection-asymmetric wormholes supported by positive energy thin-shells

**Authors:** Merce Guerrero, Gonzalo J. Olmo, Diego Rubiera-Garcia

**Journal ref:** none

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00840 - arXiv abstract listing

## Abstract

We consider reflection-asymmetric thin-shell wormholes within Palatini $f(\mathcal{R})$ gravity using a matching procedure of two patches of electrovacuum space-times at a hypersurface (the shell) via suitable junction conditions. The conditions for having (linearly) stable wormholes supported by positive-energy matter sources are determined. We also identify some subsets of parameters able to locate the shell radius above the event horizon (when present) but below the photon sphere (on both sides). We illustrate with an specific example that such two photon spheres allow an observer on one of the sides of the wormhole to see another (circular) shadow in addition to the one generated by its own photon sphere, which is due to the photons passing above the maximum of the effective potential on its side and bouncing back across the throat due to a higher effective potential on the other side. We finally comment on the capability of these double shadows to seek for traces of new gravitational physics beyond that described by General Relativity.

## Why this matters here

Guerrero, Olmo and Rubiera-Garcia build reflection-asymmetric thin-shell wormholes in Palatini f(R) gravity by matching two different electrovacuum patches across a shell using the appropriate junction conditions, and they determine when the resulting configuration is linearly stable and supported by positive-energy matter. This is the strongest existence result in the asymmetric direction: an asymmetric junction can carry a layer that satisfies the energy conditions, in a theory that is not General Relativity. It is important to state precisely what that does and does not give us. It gives a physically acceptable layer, not the absence of a layer, and that distinction is the entire question the project is trying to settle. The observational part, where the two sides have different photon spheres so that an observer on one side sees a second shadow ring produced by photons that cross the throat and bounce back off the higher effective potential on the far side, is a clean geometric illustration of how asymmetric matching produces partial transmission and partial return. That mechanism is a useful analogue for our two crossings, where the exit face need not behave like the entrance face. The Palatini setting means the result does not transfer to General Relativity unchanged.
