# Generalized junction conditions for discontinuous metrics

**Authors:** J. A. Silva, F. C. Carvalho, Antonio R. G. Garcia

**Journal ref:** none

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07936 - arXiv abstract listing

## Abstract

In this work, the Darmois-Israel junction formalism is extended to the case of discontinuous metrics within the framework of Colombeau algebras of generalized functions. This formulation provides a mathematically consistent treatment of nonlinear operations involving singular quantities, such as products and derivatives of distributions. By relaxing the usual continuity condition on the metric, the generalized junction conditions naturally include higher-order singular terms in the curvature and in the surface energy-momentum tensor. These additional contributions represent new geometric degrees of freedom associated with genuine discontinuities in the space-time geometry. The resulting formalism recovers the traditional Darmois-Israel conditions as a limiting case, while offering a coherent extension applicable to geometric boundaries and abrupt transitions in space-time.

## Why this matters here

This paper extends the Darmois-Israel formalism to the case where the metric itself is discontinuous, using Colombeau algebras so that products and derivatives of singular objects are well defined. That is exactly the situation at our threshold, where the two sides carry different signatures and no common continuous metric exists in the naive sense. The central result is negative for us: relaxing the continuity requirement on the metric does not remove the surface layer, it makes it worse, because the generalized junction conditions then contain higher-order singular terms in both the curvature and the surface energy-momentum tensor, beyond the single delta term of standard Israel theory. The authors present these extra terms as new geometric degrees of freedom attached to genuine discontinuities, and recover the classical Darmois-Israel conditions only as a limiting case in which the discontinuity is switched off. Read against our slab calculation, the Colombeau regularization is the formal counterpart of our finite thickness profile, and the paper says that what survives the limit is a layer with more structure, not less. This is the most direct recent statement that discontinuous matching cannot buy layer-free transmission. It should be cited wherever the work claims the weak condition is cheap.
