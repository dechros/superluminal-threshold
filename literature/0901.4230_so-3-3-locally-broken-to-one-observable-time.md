# The Dirac equation and a non-chiral electroweak theory in six dimensional spacetime from a locally gauged SO(3,3) symmetry group

**Authors:** C. A. Dartora, G. G. Cabrera

**Journal:** unpublished preprint (2009)

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.4230

## Abstract

A toy model for the electroweak interactions (without chirality) is proposed in a six dimensional spacetime with 3 timelike and 3 spacelike coordinates. The spacetime interval ds^2 = dx_mu dx^mu is left invariant under the symmetry group SO(3,3). We obtain the six-dimensional version of the Dirac gamma matrices, Gamma_mu, and write down a Dirac-like lagrangian density, L = i psibar Gamma^mu nabla_mu psi. The spinor psi is decomposed into two Dirac spinors, psi_1 and psi_2, which we interpret as the electron and neutrino fields, respectively. In six-dimensional spacetime the electron and neutrino fields are then merged in a natural manner. The SO(3,3) Lorentz symmetry group must be locally broken to the observable SO(1,3) Lorentz group, with only one observable time component, t_z. The t_z-axis may not be the same at all points of the spacetime and the effect of breaking the SO(3,3) spacetime symmetry group locally to an SO(1,3) Lorentz group is perceived by the observers as the existence of the gauge fields. The origin of mass may be attributed to the remaining two hidden timelike dimensions. We interpret the origin of mass and gauge interactions as a consequence of extra time dimensions, without the need of the so-called Higgs mechanism for the generation of mass. Further, we are able to give a geometric meaning to the electromagnetic and non-abelian gauge symmetries.

## Why this matters here

This is the closest match in the literature to the exact configuration our project studies: three timelike and three spacelike coordinates, with a mechanism that reduces the three times to one. The reduction is a local symmetry breaking SO(3,3) to SO(1,3), and its content is the selection at each spacetime point of a single observable time direction, called t_z, out of the three available. Two consequences are established and both bear directly on our question. First, the choice of t_z is a local orientation, so the t_z axis need not be the same at different points, and the failure of this orientation to be constant is exactly what the observer perceives as gauge fields, that is the connection that compensates the pointwise reorientation among the times. Second, the two times that are not selected do not vanish; their contribution to the six dimensional Dirac operator appears in the surviving four dimensional theory as mass terms, so the hidden orientation among the times becomes rest mass downstairs. The six dimensional Clifford algebra with Gamma_mu satisfying the SO(3,3) relations and the decomposition psi = (psi_1, psi_2) into two four component Dirac spinors shows how a single spinor carrying an orientation among the three times splits into two distinct particle species after reduction. For our model this supplies both the group theory of the reduction and a concrete answer to what the orientation of a three component energy vector converts into: a mass and a gauge charge, with the pointwise variation of the chosen time axis carrying the rest of the information.
